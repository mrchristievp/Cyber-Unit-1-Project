Case File 01: The Friday Afternoon Incident

A school security team has received several suspicious reports. Determine what the evidence supports — and what still needs verification.

STATUS: ACTIVE Initial triage in progress

CASE BRIEFING

Your Assignment

Junior Security Analyst

What happened?

At approximately 2:30 PM on Friday, school staff and students began reporting suspicious messages and unusual account activity. The technology team does not yet know whether all of the reports are connected.

Your team has been given the available incident evidence. Analyze it carefully before deciding what you think occurred.

Questions your team must answer

  • What facts are directly supported by the evidence?
  • What conclusions are likely, but not yet proven?
  • Which events may be unrelated?
  • What should the school do immediately?
  • What should investigators verify next?

Analyst rule

Do not force every clue into one story. Some artifacts may support the same attack chain. Others may be unrelated, ambiguous, or incomplete.

RESPONSE DESK

Prepare Your Security Briefing

1. Executive Summary

State what you believe happened and how serious the incident is. Separate confirmed facts from inference.

2. Timeline

Identify the key events in order. Cite artifact numbers that support each step.

3. Evidence & Findings

Explain your strongest evidence. Classify major conclusions as known, likely, possible, or needing verification.

4. Attack Chain

Show how the incident may have progressed. Do not include a step unless you can support it with evidence.

5. Response Plan

Separate actions for the first 15 minutes from longer-term prevention and policy changes.

6. AI + Human Judgment

Evaluate the automated incident analysis. Identify what it gets right, what it assumes, and what humans still need to verify.

Presentation standard: Every important claim should point back to evidence. Be ready to answer, “What evidence supports that?”
